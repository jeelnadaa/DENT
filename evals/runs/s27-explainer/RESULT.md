# S27 explainer run: RESULT

Prompt: `/dent:video a 60-second explainer of how passkeys work, for people who are tired of passwords. Narrated. Make it genuinely memorable.`
Skill: DENT 2.5.1 from this worktree (`skills/dent/`), followed through `commands/video.md` and every reference it loads. Scripted answers: Polished; "Let me pick the ingredients" (calm and precise · kinetic type + custom title lettering · morph and match + cinematic camera · illustration + data visualisation · narration + score + SFX · never: padlocks, hooded hackers); local Qwen3-TTS; code-built; music in code; 16:9.

## Outcome
- **Delivered:** `film-16x9.mp4` (63.5 s, 1080p60; 23.9 MB master not committed, `film-16x9-preview.mp4` committed), narration (`vo/`), captions (`film-16x9.srt/.vtt`), `VIDEO.md` (menu answers, 2 bibles, chosen one, originality guard, escalation moves, fact basis), `QA.md`, `SCORECARD.md` (self-review), contact sheets (`contact-sheet-1s.jpg`, `qa-sheet.jpg`), style frame B.
- **video_qa --kind explainer:** all measured checks pass on the second render (first render failed *variety*: 26.8 s unchanged; fixed in the colour script). Frozen 2 %, longest hold 0.9 s, variety 7.8 s, −14.0 LUFS, captions ≤ 42 × 2. Layout check: 0 / 0 / 0.
- **Was it original?** Yes against DENT's launch film (8 axes, no listed devices) and the user's never-list. **Was it exceptional?** Partly. The opening, the pen-line lettering, the `bank.example` / `bank.examp1e` climax and the callback work; the middle act (scenes 5–7) repeats one phone/card/wire layout, energy is low, and the climax "silence" is only a 4.6 dB dip. Self-score 7.1/10, below the 8 target. Sound was never heard by a person.
- **Did an ordinary prompt yield it?** Only with substantial agent-built tooling the skill doesn't provide: an STT checker, a narration→timeline bridge and a score style + SFX set (items 1, 5 and 6 below). Without those, the "Whisper word timestamps" invariant can't be met as written.
- **Time:** ~3 h wall clock, mostly TTS (2 × ~9 min plus a line re-render) and two ~10–19 min renders.

## Where the skill was unclear, missing, contradictory or slow
Ranked by how much each one cost or risked the result.

### Missing tools the instructions assume
1. **No speech-to-text tool, though the skill requires an STT pass.** `voice-and-narration.md` §5.4/§7 say to check every name in a Whisper transcript and the kit's invariant 1 says to put events on "Whisper word timestamps", but `tts.py` has no `stt`/`check` subcommand and nothing in `scripts/` produces word times. `doctor.py` reports `whisper: not found` even though `faster_whisper` is installed in the registered media folder (`D:\SKILLS\.dent-media\eval`). I wrote `stt_check.py` (per-line WER, pace, word times) and `build_timeline.py` (word times → `timeline.json` events). faster-whisper on CUDA failed (`cublas64_12.dll` missing), so it ran on CPU; nothing in the skill mentions that path.
   *Fix:* `tts.py check --in vo/lines` (WER + inserted-word detection + pace) and `tts.py words narration.wav → narration.words.json`; `doctor.py` should look for faster-whisper inside the registered engine home.
2. **The STT pass found more than names.** Take 1 had TTS hallucinations: an inserted "Haha" before line 3 and a whole inserted phrase in line 6 (heard as "Clear side? Bortle point."). The skill frames STT as a pronunciation check for acronyms and names; it should say *read every line's transcript for inserted or dropped words* — these would have shipped otherwise (nobody listens in an unattended run).
3. **Common words get mispronounced too, and inconsistently.** "passkey" was fine in 9 lines and came out as "Pascy" (take 1) / "past ski" (take 2) in the same line both times. §5.4 only warns about acronyms and invented names. Respelling to "pass-key" fixed it, but `tts.py say` writes the respelt text to `line_NNN.txt`, which `post` uses for captions, so I had to hand-edit the `.txt` to keep the captions correct. *Fix:* a `spoken|shown` syntax in the script (or a pronunciation map) so captions never carry respellings.
4. **Re-rendering one line is manual.** There is no `--line N` for `say`; I rendered into a new folder with `--text` and copied `line_001.wav` over `line_008.wav`. Each Qwen3 run took ~8.5 min for 10 lines on the 8 GB GPU with no progress output.
5. **No bridge from narration to the kit's timeline.** `tts.py post` writes line-level `narration.timing.json` and an SRT; the kit's `timeline.json` wants `scenes`, `events`, `cues` and is built around a beat grid (`t = n * 60 / bpm`). Nothing converts one to the other, and the kit's README never shows a narrated workflow beyond `score.py --voice`. Converting SRT → `cues` I first lost the 2-line breaks (burned captions became one ~80-character line, breaking §7's ≤ 42 × 2 rule); caught on the contact sheet. *Fix:* `build_timeline.py`-style helper in the kit (narration words → events, line starts → scenes, SRT → cues with line breaks).

### Things I had to invent that the skill should provide
6. **No score preset fits "calm and precise".** `score.py` offers `pulse`, `cinematic`, `playful`, `minimal`; the README says to "shape them to the bible" but there is no extension point. I added a `drafting` arrangement and nine SFX kinds (pen, failed pen, paper slide, ticks, digit chatter, glass chime, hollow knock, cut hit, two-part sonic logo) by editing this film's copy. The SFX loop only honours `dur` for `type` and `riser`, so I added a `SFX_DUR` table. *Fix:* a documented `register_sfx` / `register_style` hook, and `dur` honoured for every kind.
7. **Explainers aren't products, but the gates are written for products.** The three-question test and "product truth ≥ 60 %" (`video-types-and-craft.md` §2b) don't fit a technology explainer; I wrote an equivalent (what is it / why safer / how to get it). The explainer genre bar (§5) has no fact-checking step at all, yet accuracy is the main risk of an explainer. I fact-checked against the bundled MWG passkey guides (`passkey-registration`), which turned out to be the most useful thing MWG contributed to a render-only page. *Fix:* an explainer row: "every claim checked against a primary source and listed in VIDEO.md".
8. **Word budget vs. film length.** "60 s ≈ 150 words" (two places) ignores the lead, inter-line gaps, the climax pause and a ≥ 2 s end card. Take 1 (153 words, instruction "unhurried") ran **73 s**; Qwen3 paced at 2.3 words/s. Take 2 needed 140 words + "natural conversational pace" to land at 59.9 s of narration and a 63.5 s film. *Fix:* "for a 60 s narrated film budget ~130–140 words; delivery instructions like 'unhurried' slow Qwen3 by ~15 %".
9. **Style frames for each bible.** video.md step 4 asks for one rendered style frame per direction, but the kit has no style-frame page; frame A is effectively the film, frame B needed its own page (`styleframe-b.html`). `render.py stills --page <name>` made this workable — worth stating in the README.
10. **Pen/overlay layers and the camera.** The kit's `livingFrame()` moves `#cam` only; anything outside it (the kit's `#wipe`, my pen overlay) does not follow the camera push, so lines drift off the shapes they should trace. Found on the contact sheet; fixed by giving the overlay the camera transform. Worth one line in the kit.
11. **`palette.py make --neutral cool` returns exactly the kit's placeholder paper and ink** (`#F6FAFC` / `#111619`). Following the tools faithfully re-creates the placeholder ground the originality guard forbids. I shifted the vellum to `#EEF2F6` (logged as Override).
